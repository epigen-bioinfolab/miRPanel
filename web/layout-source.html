Tool 1 of 5 Seed analyser
The question it answers
Does this gene's sequence contain an exact match to the miRNA's seed — the 7 nucleotides (positions 2–8) that do most of the pairing work? A perfect seed match is the classic definition of a binding site, and the rule most target-prediction methods were originally built around.
What it gives you
A site type, and exactly where it sits
Can look at
5′UTR CDS 3′UTR
How to read it

8mer > 7mer‑m8 > 7mer‑A1 > 6mer. An 8mer is the textbook site. A 6mer on its own is weak — most 6mers happen by chance alone.

Tool 2 of 5 RNAhybrid · 2.1.2-8
The question it answers
Could the miRNA and this sequence actually fold together and bind — even where the seed itself isn't a perfect match? Instead of applying a fixed rule, RNAhybrid searches the whole transcript for the single most energetically favourable pairing, closer to how the two strands would really behave inside a cell.
What it gives you
Minimum free energy (ΔG), in kcal/mol
Can look at
Whole transcript
How to read it

More negative means a tighter fold. We only keep hits below −15 kcal/mol; below −25 is genuinely tight. Wobble (G:U) pairs are allowed, so it catches sites the seed analyser refuses.

Tool 3 of 5 TargetScan · v8.0
The question it answers
Taking the sequence around the site into account, how strongly would this gene actually be repressed — and has that exact site been kept in place across species more often than chance alone would explain? A site conserved through evolution is one natural selection has actively protected, which is strong indirect evidence that it's real.
Context++ score
Roughly 0 to −1
Conservation
Conserved sites & PCT
Can look at
3′UTR only
How to read it

More negative context++ = stronger predicted repression (−0.50 is strong, −0.02 is close to nothing). PCT is the probability a site was conserved because a miRNA actually uses it — 0.85 is strong, and no conserved site means no PCT at all. Conservation is counted per gene on its own transcript, so it can't be pinned to one row in the site table.

Tool 4 of 5 miRTarBase · 10.0
The question it answers
Has this miRNA–gene pair actually been tested at the bench, and what did the experiment find? Every other tool here predicts; miRTarBase tells you what has already been measured directly, and how rigorously it was measured.
What it gives you
Experiment counts, and the papers behind them
Can look at
Every gene
How to read it

Strong = a reporter assay, western blot or qPCR — one pair, tested deliberately. Weak = CLIP-seq, microarray, pSILAC — thousands of pairs screened at once. Strong evidence outweighs weak; watch for experiments that reported no effect.

Tool 5 of 5 scanMiR 1.16.0
The question it answers
Combining sequence, structure and real biochemical binding data into a single score, how tightly would this miRNA actually bind here — across the 5′UTR, CDS and 3′UTR all at once? Rather than a rule-based cutoff, this is a model trained directly on RNA binding measurements, the closest thing here to one unified answer.
What it gives you
A binding affinity (log Kd) per site, plus a repression estimate per transcript
Can look at
5′UTR CDS 3′UTR
How to read it

A CNN model trained directly on real biochemical binding data (RNA bind‑n‑seq), not just sequence conservation. More negative log Kd = tighter, more confident binding. Non‑canonical sites are included but flagged low‑confidence, so canonical sites are the safer default view.
